Yourself First.

Before you write a caption, build a funnel, or plan a launch — come back to yourself. The most authentic marketing comes from practitioners who are deeply connected to why they do their work. That connection only comes from within.

Marketing from a dysregulated place produces content that feels forced, performances that exhaust you, and messaging that does not land. Marketing from a grounded place produces content that is honest, specific, and magnetic — because it comes from a person who genuinely knows who they are and what they have to offer.

Honour your practice first

Meditate. Walk. Move. Breathe. Journal. Whatever brings you back to yourself — do that before you open a content calendar or write a single word of marketing copy. Your practice is your most important brand asset.

Nourish before you broadcast

Marketing is an act of service — sharing what you have with people who need it. You can only serve from fullness. Come to your marketing from a place of genuine abundance, not from scarcity or hustle energy.

Why do you do this work? Not the professional answer. The real one. The reason you would do this even if nobody was watching.
Who are you when you are at your best? Not your credentials. The quality of your presence, your perspective, your way of being with people.

01

Brand Foundation

Positioning, ideal client, your story and voice. Everything in marketing flows from the clarity you find here — or falls apart without it.

What brand actually means

Your brand is not your logo, your colour palette, or your Instagram aesthetic. It is the intersection of what you are genuinely good at, who you are here to serve, and what you believe about your work that most people in your space do not say out loud.

Without that clarity, every piece of content you create is a guess. With it, every piece of content is an invitation — and the right people recognise themselves in it immediately.

The positioning sentence

Complete this clearly: "I help [specific person] to [specific transformation] through [your unique approach]." Not a category of person — one real human being. Not a general transformation — one felt shift. Your specific lens. If you cannot complete this sentence, your marketing will never land with the right person.

Your positioning sentence Write it badly first. You can refine it later.

Collaborations, Partnerships
& Showing Up

The strategies most wellness practitioners never fully use — and the ones that often fill retreats faster than anything you post on social media.

Collaborations with other practitioners

A collaboration puts you in front of someone else's warm, trusted audience. One well-chosen collaboration can fill a retreat faster than a month of solo social media posts.

Type 01

Audience swap

Guest in their newsletter, takeover their Instagram for a day, or be featured in their content. They do the same for you. Low effort, direct access to warm audience.

Type 02

Co-hosted event

Host a workshop or retreat together. Each markets to their own audience. Both audiences attend. Both grow. Works best when modalities complement each other.

Type 03

Affiliate / referral

Offer a commission or reciprocal referral arrangement. They send participants your way, you send theirs. Formalise with a simple written agreement.

How to approach a collaboration

Identify practitioners whose audience overlaps with yours but who do not offer the same thing. Reach out with a specific, mutual offer — not "let's work together" but "I'm hosting a retreat in September for [your audience] and I'd love to offer your community early access. Here's how I'd support your work in return." Make the value exchange clear and concrete upfront.

Attending other events — the most overlooked strategy

The fastest way to build a warm audience is to show up in spaces where your ideal clients already are. Attend wellness festivals, yoga teacher trainings, breathwork gatherings, women's business events, and professional networking events. Not to sell — to connect, to be visible, to become part of the community you serve.

Micro-influencer partnerships

2,000–20,000 followers, deep trust

The most effective partnerships are not with mega-influencers — they are with micro-influencers in your specific niche who have genuine trust with their audience. Offer a complimentary spot at your event in exchange for authentic content creation. Only partner with people who would genuinely attend whether or not they were invited — audiences can tell the difference immediately.

AI in Your Marketing

How to use AI tools effectively without losing the only thing about your content that is irreplaceable — your voice, your story, your perspective.

The golden rule

You are always the author. AI is always the editor. The ideas, experiences, and perspective are yours. AI helps you shape them. The moment you ask AI to create from nothing, you lose the only thing about your content that is genuinely irreplaceable — you.

The voice test — run this before publishing

Would I say this out loud to a client?If it sounds stiff or formal in a way you never actually speak — rewrite it in your real voice.
Does it contain a specific detail only I would know?A memory, a place, a client conversation, an observation. Add at least one.
Does it take a clear position?AI defaults to balanced and non-committal. Your content should have a point of view.
Could any other wellness practitioner post this?If yes — add the thing that makes it specifically yours.
Does it have my rhythm?Read it out loud. Does it sound like how you actually talk?

AI phrases to always remove: "In today's fast-paced world" · "it's important to note" · "delve into" · "comprehensive" · "holistic approach" (when generic) · "journey" (overused) · "tapestry" · "vibrant" · "leverage" · any sentence beginning "Certainly!"

05

Email Marketing
& Automated Flows

Your highest-converting channel — and the one most wellness practitioners use least effectively. Build these once. They work for you indefinitely.

Why email outperforms social for conversions

Your email list is the only audience you own. A list of 300 engaged subscribers will outperform 5,000 social media followers for event sign-ups. The open rate on a well-written email to a warm list is 40–60%. The organic reach of an Instagram post is 3–8%. Prioritise your list above all other channels.

The four flows every wellness practitioner needs

Build these once in MailerLite, ConvertKit, or your chosen platform. They run automatically in the background while you focus on your work.

Flow 01 — Triggered: someone joins your list

Welcome Sequence — 5 emails over 14 days

Introduces you, your story, your beliefs, your work, and your community. Moves a stranger from "who is this?" to "I trust this person." This is your most important email flow — build it first.

Email by email:

Email 1 · Day 0
Deliver + introduce. What they signed up for, delivered immediately. One paragraph about who you are — not your CV, your real story. One invitation to reply with something specific ("What brought you here?").
Email 2 · Day 2
Your story. Why you do this work. Not the professional version — the personal one. The moment you knew this was your path. What changed for you that you now create for others.
Email 3 · Day 5
One clear belief. A specific perspective on your field that is yours. Something that might polarise slightly — people who agree will become loyal, people who disagree will unsubscribe. Both outcomes are good.
Email 4 · Day 9
Social proof. A transformation story — client testimonial, retreat experience, or your own. Specific, honest, and real. This is the email that moves warm people to ready.
Email 5 · Day 14
The invitation. What they can do next — book a call, join your community, sign up for your next event. One clear CTA. No manufactured urgency — just a genuine offer.
Flow 02 — Triggered: someone purchases a digital guide

Guide Buyer Flow — 3 emails over 7 days

Delivers the guide, expands on the content with something personal, invites the natural next step.

Email 1 · Immediate
Delivery + welcome. Guide download link. Brief welcome to the Mindful Earth community. One sentence on what to expect next.
Email 2 · Day 3
Go deeper. Share one thing related to the guide topic that did not fit in the guide itself — a personal story, an insight, a practice. This is the email that creates loyalty.
Email 3 · Day 7
Natural next step. Introduce the logical next offering — a related guide, a clarity call, an upcoming event. Soft invitation, not a hard sell.
Flow 03 — Triggered: event interest opt-in

Event Interest Flow — 4 emails before bookings open

Builds trust and desire between when someone expresses interest and when bookings open. The people who have been through this flow convert at much higher rates when you open bookings.

Email 1
Thank you + what to expect. They are on the list. Here is what you are building. Here is why you created this specific event. One sentence on when bookings open.
Email 2 · 1 week
Teach around the topic. Share something genuinely useful related to your event theme — no mention of the event itself. Pure value. This is the email that builds the most trust.
Email 3 · 2 weeks
Social proof. A past participant story, or your own experience at a similar event. Who this is for and what changes. Specific and honest.
Email 4 · Bookings open
The announcement. Bookings are open. Here is the event, who it is for, what they will experience, and how to book. One clear link. Early bird offer if applicable.
Flow 04 — Triggered: someone attends your event

Post-Event Nurture Flow — 3 emails over 2 weeks

Integration support, testimonial request, and next offering introduction. This is the flow that turns one-time attendees into long-term clients.

Email 1 · 24hr
Thank you. Personal, specific, reference something from the event. Include a resource relevant to integration — a practice, a reading, a reflection prompt.
Email 2 · 3 days
Integration support + testimonial ask. How are they doing? One tip for sustaining what opened at the event. Ask gently: "Would you share 2–3 sentences about your experience that I can use on my website?"
Email 3 · 2 weeks
What comes next. Introduce your next event or 1:1 offering. They are your warmest audience — give them first access or an early bird option.

6-email event launch sequence

Email 1

Subject: Something is coming.

Lead with your story — why you are hosting this, not what it is. The logistics come second. End with one link and one ask.

Email 2

Subject: Is this for you?

Be specific. Name the person. Name the situation. The right people feel seen. The wrong people self-select out — both outcomes are good.

Email 3

Subject: What will actually change.

Concrete outcome. Use a testimonial if you have one. Make it honest — not glossy.

Email 4

Subject: You might be thinking...

Address the real reasons people hesitate — cost, time, not sure if it is right for them. Be empathetic and direct. This email converts the most hesitant leads.

Email 5

Subject: X spots remaining.

Honest update on availability. Only use real urgency. If you have limited spots, say how many. If not, use the closing date instead.

Email 6

Subject: This closes [day].

Final email. Direct and warm. One link. Say goodbye gracefully to those who are not joining this time.

06

Building Your
Sales Funnel

A sales funnel is the journey someone takes from first discovering you to becoming a paying client. Design it intentionally — or leave it to chance.

The wellness funnel — built on trust

In wellness, the funnel is built on trust — not urgency, scarcity tactics, or aggressive follow-up. People need to see you consistently, understand what you do, feel aligned with your values, and believe you can help them specifically — before they commit to attending your retreat or booking your services.

The four stages

Awareness: Someone discovers you for the first time — social media, word of mouth, Pinterest, a collaboration, an event you attended.

Interest: They follow you, sign up to your list, save your content. Warm but not ready. Keep showing up, keep being specific, keep demonstrating you understand their problem.

Desire: They have followed long enough to trust you. A testimonial, a well-told story, or seeing someone they know attend tips them into "I want this."

Action: They book, buy, or enquire. Make the action simple — one clear link, one clear price, one clear next step.

This requires patience — and it is the single biggest differentiator between retreat hosts who fill easily and those who scramble at the end.

The inner work of selling

Selling is service

Selling your work is not self-promotion. It is telling the person who needs what you have that it exists. Every time you avoid posting, undercharge, or make your offer unclear — you are making the decision for someone else that they do not need this. That is not humility. It is a form of withholding.

The most common mindset blocks: "I don't want to be pushy." "My work should speak for itself." "I'm not ready yet." "I need more followers first." None of these are true. All of them keep good practitioners invisible.

What story am I telling myself about why people are not booking?
What would I tell a friend who was hesitating to share their work publicly?
