Yourself First.

Before you open a budget spreadsheet, before you check venue availability, before you write a single word of marketing copy — come back to yourself. Your event will only be as grounded as you are.

The most meaningful events are not born from hustle. They are born from stillness — from a practitioner who knows why they are doing this, who they are doing it for, and what they genuinely have to offer. That clarity only comes from within.

This section is not a step you skip to get to the "real work." This is the foundation. The rest of the guide builds on it.

Your practice before your business

Meditate. Breathe. Walk barefoot. Move your body. Journal. Sit in silence. Whatever practice returns you to yourself — do that before you do any of this. You are the container for your event. Tend to the container first.

Nourish yourself before nourishing your business

Eat something real. Sleep enough. Be in nature. Connection to your own nervous system is the most important planning tool you have. A regulated practitioner creates regulated spaces.

Your intention for this event Take a few breaths. Let an answer arise rather than forcing one. What wants to be created through you?

01

Vision & Business Case

Before logistics, before pricing, before anything practical — you need to know why you are doing this and who it is genuinely for.

Why events grow your business

Events as a business development tool

Most wellness practitioners think of events as a service offering — something they do for their clients. The shift that changes everything is starting to think about events as a business development strategy — something they do for their business, that also deeply serves their clients.

A single well-run retreat can generate more referrals, testimonials, and follow-on clients than six months of social media content. The people who attend become advocates. The photos become marketing assets. The experience becomes your most compelling case study.

The business flywheel

Run a great event → participants share their experience → word of mouth brings new people → new people attend your next event → they refer their network → your events compound over time. Each event you host makes the next one easier to fill. This is not a side hustle. It is a business system.

Developing your concept

Before anything practical, you need a clear concept. Not a vague intention — a specific, articulated vision of what this event is, who it is for, and what they will leave with.

What is it

The Event Concept

Your concept is the combination of format, theme, and atmosphere. Good concepts are specific enough that the right person immediately knows it is for them — and the wrong person immediately knows it is not.

Why it matters

The Transformation

What changes for someone who attends? Not the activities — the actual shift. The transformation is what you are selling. Everything else is the container that holds it.

What makes it yours

Your Unique Lens

Why are you the right person to host this? Your story and your lens are your differentiator. What experience do you bring that nobody else can replicate?

The Scaling Framework

Every format has a different risk profile, resource requirement, and revenue potential. Know which one you are building before you start planning.

Start smaller than you think you need to. A workshop with 10 people done exceptionally well will do more for your business than a retreat with 20 done adequately. Master the smaller format first. Scale when you have the systems, the team, and the testimonials.

Format deep-dives

Entry point

Workshop / Half-Day

Low risk, low complexity, high learning value. Perfect for testing your concept, building a local audience, and creating content. Price: CA$25–150 per person.

Key focus: one clear skill or experience. One facilitator. One room. Keep it tight.

Next level

Full-Day Event

Requires proper venue, catering, and programme structure. Participants need a full arc — opening, active work, integration, closing. Price: CA$75–250 per person.

Key additions: catering, photography, welcome pack, proper sound.

Growth format

Weekend Retreat

The sweet spot for most practitioners. Enough time for real transformation, manageable complexity, strong revenue. Price: CA$500–2,500 per person.

Key additions: accommodation logistics, meals, evening programming, emergency protocol.

Premium

Full-Week Retreat

High-touch, high-investment, deeply immersive. Requires experienced facilitation, a support team, and deep pre-event participant communication. Price: CA$2,000–6,000+.

Key additions: dedicated retreat centre, support team, medical protocols, daily programme design.

Advanced

International Retreat

Everything in a full-week retreat, plus: international transfers, travel insurance requirements, visa considerations, local supplier relationships, on-the-ground contingency plans. Price: CA$3,500–10,000+.

Do not attempt without at least 3–4 successful domestic retreats first.

Community format

Day Festival

Multi-facilitator, multi-stage event. Requires event management skills, a strong team, vendor management, ticketing, and marketing budget. Different revenue model. Price: CA$25–150 per ticket.

Key skill: logistics management and delegation, not just facilitation.

03

Budget & Financials

Know your numbers before you commit to anything. A budget built on your minimum viable participants protects you — and a financially sustainable event is what makes the next one possible.

Pricing frameworks

Method 01

Cost-plus pricing

Total costs ÷ minimum participants + facilitation fee + 25% margin. Safe, conservative, good for first events. Rarely reflects your full value.

Method 02

Value-based pricing

Price the outcome, not the hours. If this retreat genuinely shifts someone's life — what is that worth? Price from that answer.

Method 03

Market positioning

Premium events sell on trust and specificity. Most wellness practitioners do better with fewer, higher-priced events than many cheap ones.

The real cost of undercharging

When you charge too little: you attract participants who do not value the work, you cannot afford the quality of experience that creates transformation, and you burn out hosting events that do not sustain you. Sustainable pricing is ethical pricing.

Brand partnerships & welcome gifts

Brand partnerships elevate your event, reduce your costs, and open new audiences. Only partner with brands whose values and aesthetic genuinely fit your event — your participants will notice when something feels off.

05

Forms, Legal & Insurance

The layer that protects you, protects your participants, and ensures you can run events confidently. Build these templates once — use them forever.

This section is not optional. Running events without the right paperwork exposes you and your participants. The good news: once you have templates, they take minutes to adapt for each new event.

The six forms you need

Form 01 — Required

Registration / Sign-Up Form

Collect: full legal name, email, phone, emergency contact, dietary requirements and allergies, physical limitations relevant to activities, how they heard about you, agreement to your terms and refund policy.

Tool: Google Forms, Typeform, or JotForm. Send link in booking confirmation email.

Form 02 — Required

Liability Waiver

Participants acknowledge the physical and/or emotional nature of activities, that they are participating voluntarily, that they have disclosed relevant health conditions, and that they release you from liability. Must be signed before the event.

Get a local lawyer to review your template. One-time cost, used across all future events.

Form 03 — Required when photographing

Media Release

Participants consent to being photographed/filmed and to their images being used in your marketing materials, social media, website, and promotional content. Include an opt-out option. Verbal consent on the day is not sufficient.

Form 04 — Recommended

Health Intake Form

For events involving somatic work, breathwork, yoga, or physical practice: detailed health history, mental health conditions (participant's choice to disclose), current medications, pregnancy, recent surgeries.

Store securely. Bring a printed copy to the event. Never share without consent.

Form 05 — Recommended

Pre-Retreat Information Pack

Send 1–2 weeks before. Include: full schedule, what to bring, venue address and directions, shuttle details, emergency contacts, what to expect emotionally and physically, any pre-retreat practices.

Prepared participants arrive calmer and more open. This is one of your most important touchpoints.

Form 06 — Post-event

Feedback Form

Send within 48 hours. 5–8 questions max: overall rating, what they valued most, what could improve, would they recommend, would they attend again, testimonial question.

Use Google Forms or Typeform. Include: "May we use your response in our marketing?"

07

Day-Of & Programme Design

How you hold the day — the structure, the energy, and the principles that turn a well-planned event into one that genuinely shifts something.

Hold the space, not the agenda

Your job is not to deliver content — it is to create conditions where participants can access their own wisdom. Know when to move forward and when to let something breathe.

The opening sets everything

The first 30 minutes determines the quality of everything that follows. Land participants before you take them anywhere. Do not rush this.

Close with the same care

Integration is the difference between an experience and a transformation. Close deliberately — with gratitude, anchoring of insights, and a clear bridge back to everyday life.

08

Post-Event & Growth

What happens after the event shapes how much of the transformation actually lands — and how effectively this event builds your next one.

Post-event timeline

24 hours
Send personalised thank you email. Reference something specific from the event. Include next steps — your services, community, next event.
48 hours
Send feedback form. Request a testimonial — be specific: "Would you be willing to write 2–3 sentences about your experience?"
3–5 days
Share event photos (with consent). Post on social media. This is your most engaging content window — use it.
1 week
Settle all supplier invoices. Reconcile budget — actual vs estimated. Note what you underestimated for next time.
2 weeks
Write your own event debrief. Do this while it is fresh.
1 month
Announce your next event — this audience is your warmest. Give them first access or early bird pricing.
