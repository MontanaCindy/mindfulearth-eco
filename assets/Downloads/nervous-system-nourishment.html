Inner Body Awareness Scan

Before getting started, take a moment to reflect on where you are at within your own nervous system. Developing an awareness of where you stand is the first step to cultivating regulation.

How to do a body scan

  1. Close your eyes and begin to notice your breath — don't try to change it. Just notice. Is it shallow? Does it feel stuck? Are you breathing into your belly or chest?
  2. Slowly deepen your inhales and exhales at your own pace. Take your time.
  3. Once your breath deepens, begin scanning your body — feet, legs, torso, up into your head. Don't rush.
  4. Ask yourself: how is my nervous system feeling today? Do you feel stuck, stressed, anxious? Get curious.
What did you notice in your body scan? Be honest and non-judgemental — there are no wrong answers here.

Intention Setting Practice

Now that you have a better understanding of where you're at, take some time to create an intention for your journey through this workbook. An intention is not a goal. Do not attach an expectation or evaluation to it. Your intention is to work towards aligning yourself with it over time.

All About Nervous System
Regulation & Nourishment

Detailed knowledge about how the nervous system works, what nervous system regulation is, and how it impacts your overall well-being.

With Montana

The Nervous System

The Nervous System is responsible for all of our human functions — from thinking, problem solving, and voluntary movements to involuntary functions and digestion.

Central Nervous System

Contains the brain and spinal cord. The brain is the control center that processes information, interprets sensory input, and coordinates the body's responses.

Autonomic Nervous System

Responsible for involuntary responses like breathing, heart rate, hormones, and digestion. Split into sympathetic (fight/flight) and parasympathetic (rest/digest).

Enteric Nervous System

A complex network of neurons within the gastrointestinal tract. Plays a crucial role in regulating digestion — sometimes called the "second brain."

Sympathetic — Fight or Flight

Prepares the body for action in response to stress or danger. Heart rate increases, respiration increases, pupils dilate, and digestion is inhibited to send energy elsewhere. Useful for taking action — but costly when chronically activated.

Parasympathetic — Rest & Digest

Promotes relaxation, conserves energy, and restores the body after a stressor. Heart rate and respiration slow, digestion activates, nutrients are absorbed. Essential for recovery and healing.

Nervous System Responses

Fight

Aggression or defensiveness. The body prepares to confront the threat. More reactive, harder to think clearly.

Flight

Instinct to escape or avoid. The body prepares to move away from the perceived threat rapidly.

Freeze

Immobility or reduced activity when the threat feels overwhelming. Can feel like shutdown or dissociation.

Fawn

People-pleasing to avoid harm. Appeasement to avoid confrontation. Often associated with helplessness.

Which response do you recognise most in yourself? Notice without judgement — this is information, not a verdict.

Signs of a Dysregulated Nervous System

This is not an exhaustive list — dysregulation is often complex and typically impacts most aspects of daily functioning.

Cognitive

Anxiety, depression, panic, feelings of overwhelm, memory problems, brain fog. When in fight/flight, energy is diverted away from the brain's optimal function.

Digestive

Nausea, diarrhea, or lack of appetite. When the sympathetic system is activated, the body struggles to digest nutrients properly.

Emotional

Less control over emotions — irritability, angry outbursts, consistent crying. Important to feel these in healthy ways so they do not impact daily life.

Physical

Increased muscle tension, aches, pains, and stiffness throughout the body. The nervous system response manifests physically.

Daily Tasks

Everyday tasks like cleaning, cooking, and self-care may feel overwhelming. Personal hygiene or home environment may suffer.

Sleep

Insomnia, constant fatigue, nightmares, or stress dreams. Sometimes the opposite — sleeping too much.

A detailed understanding of the ways that breathwork, yoga, and meditation have on our nervous system.

With Julia

How Breath Impacts the Nervous System

Shallow breathing (especially through the mouth) activates the sympathetic stress response due to the concentration of SNS receptors in the upper lobes of the lungs. Full and deep breaths that reach the lower lobes activate the parasympathetic response. Breathing through the nose allows for more air to enter the lungs due to the structure of the nasal passages and sinus cavities.

The diaphragm connects to many areas of the body and plays a vital role in the lymphatic, circulatory, and digestive systems. If we don't engage it enough, it will atrophy and lose its flexibility — this is prevented by taking deeper and fuller breaths.

Types of Breathwork

Breath of Fire

Short rapid equal inhales + exhales

Engage the diaphragm and pump the breath rhythmically. Good for gaining focus and clarity, increased lymphatic drainage, and stress reduction.

Box Breathing

Inhale 4 · Hold 4 · Exhale 4 · Hold 4

Four-part equal pattern. Promotes relaxation, slows racing thoughts, and improves concentration.

4-7-8 Breathing

Inhale 4 · Hold 7 · Exhale 8

Great for better sleep, calming the nervous system, and lowering anxiety. Hold while lungs are near full capacity.

Physiological Sigh

Double inhale · Long exhale

Breathe in to full capacity, take a short second breath in, then a long slow exhale. Immediately calms the nervous system and lowers anxiety.

Breath-Body Awareness Practice (10–15 minutes)

Begin sitting or lying down comfortably. Bring your attention to your breath. Notice how the breath enters, and what parts of your body expand with it.

  1. Imagine a balloon inside your belly — it expands with every inhale, comes back with each exhale. No pause between inhale and exhale.
  2. Move the balloon down to your pelvis — feel the pelvic floor expand towards your feet with each inhale, return with each exhale.
  3. Shift awareness to your lower ribs and mid-back. Notice the ribcage expand. Allow natural movement to arise in the spine, pelvis, and neck.
  4. Move the balloon to your upper chest and heart area. Notice how the chest rises and falls. With each inhale, the heart area starts to soften.
  5. Move to the throat and neck. Then up to the entire skull. Then slowly scan back down.
  6. When your experience naturally closes, thank yourself for this time.
Journal about your breath-body awareness experience

Breath-Walk Practice

A simple yet effective way to calm your nervous system that you can do anywhere.

  1. Begin walking slowly and steadily, paying attention to each step.
  2. Box breathe with your steps: inhale for 5 steps, hold for 5 steps, exhale for 5 steps, hold for 5 steps.
  3. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back to your steps and breath.
  4. Start with 5–10 minutes and work up over time.

Nervous System Regulation
From a Holistic Nutrition View

An exploration of the link between the parasympathetic nervous system and digestive health — with education about intuitive eating and cultivating calmness through diet.

With Em McInnes R.H.N.

"We are not only shaped by what we eat, but more intricately, by what our bodies can effectively digest and absorb."

Rest and Digest

The parasympathetic nervous system plays a vital role in creating an environment conducive to efficient digestion. When the body is in a state of relaxation, the parasympathetic nervous system becomes more active — triggered in situations of rest, after a meal, or during periods of low stress.

Holistic Nutrition emphasises mindful eating practices to encourage the parasympathetic nervous system to dominate during meals — paying attention to the sensory experience of eating, chewing thoroughly, and being present.

Healthy Digestion Practices

Smaller meals and grazing

Prevents overloading the digestive system, optimises nutrient absorption, maintains stable blood sugar, and encourages mindful eating habits.

Chew thoroughly

Initiates the breakdown process, eases the workload on the digestive system, and enhances nutrient absorption. Do not rush eating.

Stay hydrated

Water is vital for healthy digestion — it aids in breaking down food, facilitates nutrient absorption, and ensures smooth bowel movements.

Gentle cooking methods

Steaming, sautéing at medium-low heat, poaching, and simmering preserve nutrients and make food easier to digest than high-heat methods.

Natural digestive aids

Peppermint, ginger, chamomile, fennel, probiotics, lemon balm, and papaya all support digestion and align with parasympathetic activation.

Mindful meal timing

Eating your last meal 2–3 hours before bedtime supports digestion, prevents discomfort, stabilises blood sugar, and improves sleep quality.

Worksheets &
Exercises

Work through these four steps towards creating a manageable plan for nervous system regulation. Take your time — return to this section as often as you need.

"Working on the long term regulation of your nervous system is a marathon, not a sprint. It is to be done mindfully with intention and patience. Taking baby steps towards your healing is better than trying to take big leaps."

Step 01 — Identify where you are currently at

Get curious about where you're at. Answering these questions without judgement will help you develop a better understanding of your current state.

How is my nervous system feeling today?
What symptoms are surfacing?
Do I feel disconnected from my body?

Step 02 — Reflect on what has contributed to your current state

Identify the factors contributing to where you are. This exercise is not meant to overwhelm you — reflect without judgement. You are doing your best.

What is a major source of overwhelm in my life right now?
What are my lifestyle habits like?
What patterns or beliefs might be contributing?

Step 03 — Identify where you want to be

Focus on the positive feelings you'd like to welcome in, rather than what you don't want. Instead of "I don't want to feel stressed" — "I would like to feel at peace."

How would my nervous system feel daily in an ideal world?
How would I show up differently for myself?
What would be different in 6 months if I committed to this?

Step 04 — Create a manageable action plan

The most important part is coming up with a plan that feels safe and manageable. Small steps cultivate safety, self-trust, and empowerment in the healing journey.

For when you notice dysregulation in the moment — immediate tools to navigate a specific symptom as it arises.

Implemented over time with the goal of eliminating or reducing the frequency of your symptoms. Typically involves development of new habits — one at a time.
