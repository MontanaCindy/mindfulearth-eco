The Journal · Montana Cindy

The long way
home.

By Montana King · Founder, Mindful Earth

I started Mindful Earth in 2019. Not because I had it figured out. Because I needed an anchor while I figured it out.

That is the honest version of the origin story. Not a light-bulb moment or a clear vision that I executed methodically. More like: here is something I believe in, let me hold onto it while the rest of my life reorganises itself around me.

And it did reorganise. More than once. More than I could have anticipated.

Where it started

I grew up carrying things that felt too big for me. Anxiety since I was six years old — the kind that is always just underneath the surface, always looking for a reason to confirm that something is wrong. Childhood experiences that I spent years learning to name properly. A body that hurt in ways that took a long time to understand.

I found yoga when I was eighteen. That sounds like a neat narrative but it was messier than that — it was more like I found the first thing that could get me out of my head and into my body, and I held onto it. The practice became the thread I kept finding my way back to, through everything that came after.

I did my yoga teacher training in 2020. I came out of it with the particular quality of openness that happens after an intensive — where everything looks slightly different than it did before, and you are not quite sure what to do with that yet. I had a dream about mountains. The next day, my friend tagged me in a listing: a beautiful home in Canmore, Alberta. Pet friendly. The Rockies. I had never been.

I packed my car that week and drove across the country.

The van, and learning to move

What I found in Canmore was something I had not known I was looking for. The mountains are enormous in a way that is humbling rather than overwhelming — they make you small, and that smallness turns out to be a relief when you have spent your whole life trying to be bigger than your fear.

I came back from Canmore and bought a van. Converted it. Spent the next few years living in it — Ontario, the east coast, eventually Vancouver Island and Tofino. I co-facilitated my first retreat away from home on Bowen Island. Something solidified. A knowing that this work — holding space, creating containers, gathering people intentionally — was mine to do.

What nobody tells you about van life is that the freedom is real and the loneliness is also real, and they exist at the same time. You meet people everywhere and leave them everywhere. You arrive in beautiful places and feel the beauty and also feel the ache of having no one who knows your full story. I loved it anyway. I learned things in those years that I could not have learned any other way.

Meanwhile, Mindful Earth was there. Evolving alongside me. Unravelling when I unravelled. Coming back together when I did. It was always a reflection — of where I was, what I understood, what I was becoming.

The studying, and the slow building of a foundation

I was always learning. Women's health, fertility, breathwork, bodywork, somatic work, neuroscience, business, marketing. Self-help books read in vans and trailers and rented rooms in countries I was still getting my bearings in. Not because I had a curriculum but because I kept encountering the edges of what I understood and needing to go further.

The holosomatic bodywork training in England was a turning point. So was the work I did on myself — the inner child work, the nervous system regulation, the slow process of understanding what I was carrying and where it had come from. Chronic pain that taught me things about the relationship between the body and stored experience that no book had told me clearly enough.

All of it fed into the work. All of it feeds into Mindful Earth now.

The inner work we do is what brings us to the outer work. Our business, as a wellness practitioner, is a reflection of the inner journey we have made. You cannot build something grounded from an ungrounded place. The foundation has to come first.

That became the principle at the centre of everything. Not a tagline. A thing I had lived my way into believing.

The business I walked away from

At some point I built something. I was so excited. I put my whole heart into it, genuinely believed in it, worked harder on it than I had worked on almost anything. And then I realised, slowly and then all at once, that it was not right for me.

Not wrong, exactly. Just not mine. The vision did not match. The values did not fully align. And I had learned enough by then about what it costs to build something that is not truly yours — costs you in a way that is hard to recover from.

So I walked away. I knew my worth. I knew my vision. I was not willing to settle for something close when I had been working toward something real.

That decision felt enormous at the time. Now it just feels like the right thing I did when I needed to do it. Mindful Earth was waiting. It is always waiting. It has that quality — patient, persistent, not going anywhere.

The losses

I have lost people in the past few years. My cousin. My grandma — who was my person, my anchor, the one who always knew what to say, which was usually something direct and loving and slightly no-nonsense. She died in 2023, three weeks after I came home from Portugal, where I had finally gotten clear on my purpose. The timing was the kind that makes you believe in something, even when the grief is too big to see clearly.

She would hate to be the sad part of this story. She always said "buck up" — not unkindly, but in the way of someone who had lived through hard things and understood that you could either be defeated by them or you could keep going. I am keeping going. I think she would be pleased about that.

My grandpa died in December 2024, a few months after I moved to London. That one shattered me in a different way — a quieter way, if quieter is even the right word. He is with her now. I feel them both, sometimes, in the particular way that people you love do not fully leave. They are behind me. That is the only way I know how to say it.

Coming home

London happened because of my partner. I had been moving for six years — never more than six months in one place, always either arriving somewhere or leaving it. Choosing to stay was one of the stranger things I have done.

It is still strange, honestly. I am still landing. The city is enormous and fast and not particularly interested in you until you find the pockets of warmth inside it, which I am still finding. My dog helps.

But something is different now than it was in 2020 when I drove away from everything familiar. I know more about what I am building and why. I know more about what it costs and what it gives. I know that the inner work and the outer work are not separate — they are the same work moving in different directions, and you cannot do one properly without doing the other.

Mindful Earth has been alive for six years now. It has looked different in almost every one of them. Right now it looks like a platform being built for wellness practitioners — the infrastructure, the community, the tools, the education. The structural side, so that people can do what they love without needing to become a business administrator to survive.

The foundation that allows people to grow from a grounded place.

I built this because I needed it to exist and it did not. Because I had been through enough to know what practitioners need — not just the strategy, but the holding. Not just the logistics, but someone who understands the particular weight of building something meaningful in a world that does not always make that easy.

I am not finished becoming who I am supposed to be. I do not think that process ever really finishes. But I am more myself now than I have ever been. That feels like enough to build from.

One of my biggest dreams is to have retreat venues in different countries. Places I have visited enough times to love and love enough to want to share. I am working toward that, one event at a time. One practitioner supported at a time. One part of the ecosystem built at a time.

The long way home turned out to be exactly the right way. I just could not have known that from the beginning.

Mindful Earth was founded in 2019 and is still becoming what it is meant to be.
